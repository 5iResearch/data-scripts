Method & Caveats

What is being tested, and how far it can be trusted
How to read it. Turns are labelled with a zigzag on each ratio's weekly RS line, using an adaptive swing threshold (1.6× the ratio's own 13-week vol, floored at 7%). Every signal is computed causally — the regression channel is refit each week on the trailing 260 weeks — and repeat firings within 8 weeks collapse into one event.
  • Edge 13w is the metric to trust. It is the mean 13-week forward RS return after the signal minus that same ratio's unconditional average over the same horizon. It needs no labels, so it does not inherit the zigzag's assumptions. For a top signal you want it negative; for a bottom signal, positive.
  • Hit rate is the share of firings followed by a labelled turn of the right kind within 13 weeks. Recall is the share of labelled turns the signal caught beforehand. A signal can have a high hit rate and useless recall — it fires rarely but is right when it does.
  • % late is the share of firings where the nearest turn had already happened. Above 50% means the signal is a confirmation, not a warning, whatever its edge.
  • p is a randomization test: the share of 2,000 draws of the same number of random dates from the same ratio that beat the signal's forward return. It does not correct for overlapping forward windows or for testing many signals at once, so a single p below 0.05 here is weak evidence, not a discovery.
  • Nothing here is optimized. These are conventional thresholds being tested, which is the point — a fitted threshold would look better and mean less.
