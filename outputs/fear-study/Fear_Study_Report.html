Leader Selloff — Event Study

Generated September 11, 2026 · US stocks (S&P 500, Nasdaq-100 + screener list, ~2,000), BTC/ETH, sector/theme ETFs · prices since 2000, signals since mid-2005 · excess returns vs SPY from next-day close
PASS — out-of-sample (2015+), US stocks, 690 signals on 300 dates. The chosen signal (weekly RSI ≤ own 10% pct, monthly RSI ≤ own 10% pct, vol spike = VIX 30 equivalent, historic leader) beats both baselines (A: leaders on any day, B: same conditions without the leader filter) on 3m and 6m excess return. 3m: signal +5.0% vs A +0.6% / B +4.0%; 6m: signal +4.1% vs A +0.9% / B +3.3% (winsorized means; raw means 3m +4.9% vs +0.6% / +4.6%; 6m +4.2% vs +1.1% / +4.5%). 6m mean 90% CI +1.3% to +6.3%.
Disclosure: when the universe was widened to ~2,000 names the verdict was switched from plain to winsorized means (returns clipped at roughly the 1st/99th percentile), after finding spliced price histories (e.g. Chord/Oasis 2020, +59,000%) and lottery-ticket rebounds that dominated plain averages. Return windows spanning a one-day +200% jump are also excluded. Raw means are shown in every table.
How to read this. Every number is an average excess return vs SPY, entered at the close the day after the signal. Signals are capped at one per ticker per 63 trading days. "Hit" = share that beat SPY. MAE = average worst drawdown from entry within 3 months (how much pain came first). The 90% CI resamples whole months, because signals cluster in crises. Baselines A and D are averages over every stock-day, so they show no median or CI. Survivorship caveat: the universe is today's listed names, so former leaders that failed are missing; absolute levels are optimistic (more so for mid caps), and the comparisons against baselines are the part to trust.
