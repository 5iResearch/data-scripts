Study: Forward Returns From Channel Extremes

Walk-forward backtest, not in-sample: at each monthly sample date (up to 25 years of history per name), the regression is fit using only the trailing ~10 years of data available as of that date — no future prices — and R² ≥ 0.50, a positive slope, and positive trailing relative strength are all required at that date to count at all (not gated on today's estimates or Z-score). That date is then classified Bottom (Z≤-1.5) / Middle / Top (Z≥+1.5) against its own trailing-only fit, and the name's real forward return is measured 6M/12M/3Y/5Y later. Because classification never sees the future, this is a fair walk-forward read of whether the setup has actually worked historically.
Across 83 bottom-of-channel, 241 middle-of-channel, and 51 top-of-channel walk-forward observations (monthly-sampled, up to 25y lookback, R²≥0.50 names only, no look-ahead), Top-of-channel had the best (or least-negative) median forward return relative to benchmark in 3 of 4 horizons measured. At 6M: +4.2% (Bottom), +3.1% (Middle), +5.4% (Top). By 5Y: +42.4% (Bottom), +25.4% (Middle), +39.7% (Top) — that edge holds up or widens as the holding period lengthens, and the top of the channel — not the middle — was where the edge showed up most consistently.

Momentum Within the Middle

Restricted to Middle-band observations from the study above, split by whether that name's Z-score was Rising (recovering off the bottom) or Falling (fading off the top) over the trailing 63 trading days as of the sample date, using that same date's own trailing regression fit — no additional look-ahead. Answers: does a middle-of-channel name's recent direction of travel predict its forward return better than its static position does?
Restricting to the 241 middle-of-channel observations at the 6M horizon (123 with rising Z-score momentum over the trailing 63 trading days, 118 with falling), median forward return relative to benchmark at 6M was +2.0% (Rising) vs. +5.1% (Falling); by 5Y it was +28.3% (Rising) vs. +22.7% (Falling) — suggesting recent Z-score momentum direction didn't reliably predict which forward relative return was better — mean reversion showed up about as often as continuation.
